The Fundamental Gaps

Why This Is Not a Close Race

01
Every Competitor Stops at the IDE
Copilot, Cursor, Windsurf, and Amazon Q are all tools that augment human developers. They make the human faster. They never eliminate the human from the build-test-security-deploy loop. When the developer closes their laptop, work stops.

Devin is the only competitor that attempts genuine autonomy — but it opens a PR and stops. It does not run security scans. It does not load-test the result. It does not create bug tickets when tests fail. The pipeline still requires humans after every PR.

FORGE is the only platform where one-liner requirement → production-deployed, tested, security-scanned software happens without any human in the implementation loop.
02
The Model Learns. No One Else's Does.
Every competitor uses a generic model that knows nothing about your company. Your 500,000 lines of Rust, your naming conventions, your error handling patterns, your architecture decisions — invisible to Copilot on day 1 and invisible to Copilot on day 500.

FORGE trains a LoRA adapter on your codebase every week, automatically, with zero human labeling. By month 6, context windows are 95% smaller (meaning 10× faster inference), and first-pass PR approval rates reach 94%. By month 12: 97%.

The platform that knows your codebase will always outperform the platform that doesn't — by a margin that grows every week you use it.
03
Devin's Pricing Is a Trap
Devin's $20/month Core plan sounds cheap. It includes 9 ACUs — approximately 2.25 hours of active work. A single moderately complex feature easily consumes 3–5 ACUs. The $500/month Team plan yields 250 ACUs — roughly 62.5 hours of work per month for your entire team.

At a company with 10 active developers, that's 6.25 hours of AI work per developer per month. At the pace of modern software development, Devin's Team plan runs out in days.

FORGE costs $299/developer/month and includes unlimited pipeline execution. A developer's GPU droplet costs ~$94/month at 25% utilization. The rest is margin. There are no surprise credit overages.
04
Jira Is the Source of Truth. Always.
Every competitor requires you to adopt a new workflow. Devin lives in Slack. Cursor lives in VS Code. Copilot lives in your editor. You manage work in one place and AI in another.

FORGE behaves exactly like a human developer inside Jira. It dequeues QUEUED tickets, moves them to IN PROGRESS, adds comments at every stage ("Build passed. Starting tests."), creates bug tickets when tests fail, links PRs to tickets, and marks tickets COMPLETED when done.

Your product owner sees work happening in Jira — the same place they've always looked. No new dashboards to check. No new tools to learn. The team's workflow doesn't change; it just happens automatically.
05
Security Is Not Optional Tooling
No competitor runs security scanning as an integrated, blocking pipeline stage. With Copilot or Cursor, security scanning is a separate tool your team runs separately, if it remembers, manually, on its own schedule.

FORGE's Security Agent runs after every successful build — automatically — scanning for CVEs in dependencies (OSV, NVD, Snyk), hardcoded secrets (Gitleaks), SQL injection, insecure auth patterns, license violations, and SOC 2 control gaps.

Critical and High findings block the pipeline. The Code Agent is dispatched to fix them before the code can proceed. Every security finding creates a Jira ticket. Security is not a phase — it is continuous.
06
100 Developers. Zero Coordination Meetings.
With 100 developers generating features concurrently, every other platform devolves into merge conflicts, blocked PRs, and coordination overhead. This is why large teams need project managers, tech leads, and sprint planning just to avoid stepping on each other.

FORGE's Feature Dependency DAG Resolver determines what can run in parallel and what must wait — automatically. The Conflict Detector prevents any two agents from writing to the same file simultaneously. The PR Merge Orchestrator sequences merges to keep main stable.

At 100 developers, FORGE delivers 20–40% faster feature delivery through automatic parallelism — without a single coordination meeting.
